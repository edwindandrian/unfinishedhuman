If you asked me to summarise my life so far, I would give you the events: the moves, the degrees, the losses, the decisions I still argue with at night. But I have never actually *lived* an event. I have lived Tuesdays.

## The arithmetic

A life of eighty years contains roughly twenty-nine thousand days. Perhaps two hundred of them will be what we later call significant. The remainder, the overwhelming majority, are the ones in which we made breakfast, answered messages, looked out of a window.

It would be strange if the meaning of a life resided only in the two hundred.

## Attention as a moral act

Simone Weil wrote that attention is the rarest and purest form of generosity. I used to read that as a statement about other people. I now read it as a statement about days.

To pay attention to an ordinary afternoon, the particular light on a wall, the way someone says your name when they are tired, is to refuse the idea that it was only a waiting period for something better.

## Not a lesson

I want to resist turning this into advice. *Cherish every moment* is a sentence that has never once made anyone cherish anything. The truth is smaller. Some days I notice, and most days I do not. But the days I notice are the ones that remain, and they were rarely the ones that announced themselves.
