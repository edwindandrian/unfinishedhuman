For a long time I believed there would be a day on which I would feel *finished*. Not perfect, exactly. Just settled: the opinions arranged, the habits in order, the old injuries filed under a heading I could stop visiting. I imagined it the way one imagines a house after the last box is unpacked.

That day has not arrived, and I have started to suspect it was never scheduled.

## The unpacked box

Somewhere in my late twenties I began keeping a notebook of things I would "figure out eventually." Why I avoid certain conversations. Why praise unsettles me more than criticism. Why I can work for nine hours on something no one will see and cannot send a two-line email to a friend.

The list was meant to shrink. Instead it grew, and some entries changed their meaning as I changed. The question *why am I like this?* became, slowly, *what is this like?* — a quieter, more curious question, and a far more useful one.

> To describe yourself is already to be slightly behind yourself. The sentence is finished; the person is not.

## Against the finished self

We are surrounded by the grammar of completion. *Find yourself. Become your best self. Heal.* These phrases promise a destination, and a destination implies that all the time before it was a kind of delay.

But consider how little of a life is the arrival. A conversation is mostly the middle of a conversation. A friendship is mostly the ordinary Tuesday. A mind is mostly in the act of changing its mind.

If that is true, then incompleteness is not the flaw in the design. It is the design.

## What changes

None of this makes me more serene, to be honest. I still lie awake sorting things. But the sorting has a different tone. I am less like a clerk trying to close the books and more like someone tending a long, uneven garden, where some things are always coming up and some things are always going under.

I have come to like the phrase *a work in progress* less than its plainer cousin: *a person.*

So this journal. Not a record of conclusions, but of the thinking as it happens, with its false starts left visible. If something here reads as certain, assume I will disagree with it in a year. That is not inconsistency. That is the whole idea.
