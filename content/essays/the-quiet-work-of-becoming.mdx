There is a kind of progress that cannot be photographed. It happens at a desk, in the third hour of a problem that will not yield, in a room where nothing appears to be occurring.

I used to think this was the unfortunate part of ambition, the tax paid before the real thing began. I now think it is the real thing, and that the visible part, the finished piece, the announcement, is mostly a receipt.

## The invisible ratio

Ask any practitioner of anything how much of their work is seen, and they will smile before answering. A cook sees a plate leave the kitchen; she remembers four hundred mornings of onions. A translator is judged on a paragraph and haunted by a single verb.

The ratio is roughly this: for every hour in public, a hundred in private. And the private hours are not preparation. They are the substance.

## Slow is a skill

We admire speed because it is measurable. But the capacities I most want, patience with confusion, tolerance for a bad first draft, the willingness to stay with a question after it stops being interesting, are all forms of slowness.

> Interest is easy at the beginning and at the end. The craft lives in the middle, where it is merely work.

## A small practice

I have started ending each working day by writing one sentence about what I *noticed*, not what I *produced*. Some days it is trivial. *The paragraph wanted to be shorter.* *I was avoiding the hard part again.*

After a few months, the sentences began to read like a portrait of someone slowly becoming more attentive. Which, it turns out, was the thing I had been after all along. The work was only the instrument.
