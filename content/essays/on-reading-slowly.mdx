A friend once asked how many books I read a year, and I realised that I had started to answer as if it were an athletic statistic. *Thirty-one, but I'm behind.*

Behind what, exactly?

## The counted book

There is a quiet pressure now to treat reading as throughput. Lists, goals, streaks. I understand the appeal; counting is a way of making a private habit feel real. But it has a side effect: it trains us to prefer the book we can finish to the book that might finish *us*.

## The page that stops you

Every serious reader knows the experience of a sentence that halts the eye. You put the book down on your knee. You look at nothing for a minute. Whatever has just happened, it was not information.

I would trade a dozen finished books for a handful of those pauses.

## A rule of thumb

I now keep a pencil beside whatever I read, and a simple rule: when a passage makes me stop, I copy it out by hand into a notebook, and I write one line beneath it about why. Not a summary. Just a reaction, however small.

At the end of a year the notebook is slim, perhaps sixty entries. It is also, by a wide margin, the truest record of what I read.
