Marriages have vows; jobs have contracts; even neighbours have, at the very least, a shared hedge. Friendship has nothing. No one is obliged to remain your friend, and no institution will notice if you stop.

This is the beauty of it, and also the danger.

## The slow drift

Most friendships do not end. They thin. A reply takes two days, then two weeks. The plans are sincere and never quite scheduled. One day you realise you know the *outline* of a person's life but not its current weather.

I do not think this is anyone's fault. It is what happens to anything that depends entirely on being chosen, again and again, with no external reason.

## What I think we owe

Not much, really, and that is the point. Not grand gestures. Something closer to these:

- **Attention that is not performing.** To listen without preparing your own story.
- **Honesty, delivered kindly.** The friend who only agrees with you is a mirror with good manners.
- **The first message.** Someone has to send it. Often the one who waits is waiting for exactly the same permission you are.
- **Room to have changed.** People are unfinished. A friend should be allowed to be a slightly different person than the one you met.

## An ordinary Tuesday

The friendships that have lasted in my life were rarely sustained by occasions. They were sustained by small, slightly unnecessary contact: a photograph of a bad sandwich, a paragraph from a book with *this made me think of you* appended.

It sounds trivial. But if I am honest, being thought of on an unremarkable Tuesday is about the nicest thing that can happen to a person.
