There is a difference between loneliness and solitude that no dictionary quite captures. Loneliness is a room you are locked in. Solitude is a room you have chosen to enter, and can leave.

The trouble is that they look identical from the outside, and increasingly from the inside too.

## The disappearing empty hour

Not long ago, a train journey was an empty hour. So was a queue, a waiting room, a walk to the shop. These were small, unplanned solitudes, and a great deal of private thinking happened in them without anyone designing it.

Now each gap is filled before it can finish forming. I do not say this with nostalgia; I say it as someone who reaches for his phone in the lift.

## What happens in there

Left alone long enough, something odd occurs. The mind first becomes restless, then bored, then, if you can bear the boredom, *spacious*. Thoughts arrive that were waiting behind the noise. Some are unwelcome. Some are the best ideas of the year.

> Solitude is not where you go to escape yourself. It is where you go to be introduced.

## A modest proposal

I am not suggesting retreats or silent mountaintops. Only this: protect one unscheduled stretch each day. Twenty minutes. No audio, no screen, no purpose. Walk, sit, wash something.

You will probably hate it for the first week. After that, you may find that you have been missing a person you used to know quite well.
