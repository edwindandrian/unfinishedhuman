## Review

What disarms me about *Meditations* is that it was never meant to be read. It is an emperor talking to himself, and the self he addresses is irritable, easily distracted, and constantly forgetting what he knows. That is exactly why it works.

It is not a book of wisdom so much as a book of *repeated effort*. The same reminders, to be patient, to stop performing, to remember that you are brief, appear again and again, because they did not stick the first time.

## Reading notes

- Book II opens with a list of the difficult people he will meet today. It is oddly tender: he reminds himself they act from ignorance, not malice.
- The sentences are short because the thoughts were needed at speed, in a tent, on a campaign.
- Better read in small doses. I managed about three passages a morning.

## Favorite passages

> You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength.

> Waste no more time arguing about what a good man should be. Be one.
